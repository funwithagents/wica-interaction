# The end-to-end tier

**Status:** In progress

Implements [specs/e2e.md](../specs/e2e.md) and the end-to-end jobs of [specs/ci.md](../specs/ci.md): the real app on both embodiments, every module real and only the devices replaced — a virtual audio cable and the room for what the mic hears, an image file as the computer's webcam, hand photos as props in the robot's sim. Three focused scenarios and one conversation per embodiment, on the Mac and in CI. It replaces `tests-e2e/test_gesture_input_live.py`. Left out: the nightly run with the production providers, the manual Windows check, a real-room run, and an echo for the robot.

## Steps

1. **The computer** (done): the shared machinery, the config, the photos, scenarios 1–3; the `WebcamSource` fix; on the Mac, then a CI job over a PulseAudio cable.
2. **The robot** (done): the bridge's harness and its sim, the hand props, the daemon's audio check, the emotion; on the Mac with the viewer sim, then a CI job with the headless sim and EGL.
3. **Interrupt and dictation** (done): a spoken and a shown stop, a dictated note; on both bodies, on the Mac and in CI.
4. **The room and the conversation** (done): the room process; the computer's echo cancellation following the echo; one conversation per body, a test per step, taking over the separate interrupt, emotion and dictation tests; the echo check.
5. **CI on main** (done): the e2e jobs leave pull requests, since each run pays the LLM and the ASR.
6. **The computer's echo on Linux** (in progress): CI's canceller removes nothing (below). Measured, then a first fix tried: small PulseAudio buffers.

## What building it found

In the product:

- **A file as the webcam stalled after one frame.** OpenCV yields an image file once, then reads return nothing without raising, and `WebcamSource` went silent with a clean status, as a webcam unplugged mid-stream would. A device that has delivered frames and then yields none has ended, and is reopened in the same poll; one that has not yet delivered is still warming up.
- **The robot's app crashed on Linux when vision started.** MediaPipe's first import, once the robot's GStreamer media ran, aborted the process (in matplotlib's FreeType extension). `build_system` imports it before entering the body; the tier imports it at session start too, since the harness's own bridge sessions run first.
- **Echo cancellation with no echo mutes the person.** Recorded live and replayed offline: WebRTC's canceller, with nothing to learn from (a headset, or the cable), suppresses near-end speech during playback until it has heard about 8 s of the app's voice. With a room's echo it learns in a second or two. The guide now says to turn it off with headphones.
- **Dictation ends on a trigger word on the computer too**, as on the robot: in `timeout` mode the passage sometimes closed after its first sentence. `timeout` stays offered; its early close is asr-engine's, with two more findings for it — the final holding the trigger word is dropped with its text, and the substring match fires inside other words.

In the setup:

- **The person's voice plays from a process of its own.** On macOS a process that plays into the cable stalls its own capture of it, silently, for PortAudio and GStreamer alike; the room runs in a child process.
- **On Linux the robot's devices need a `device.string`.** Upstream's matcher, on PulseAudio without PipeWire, takes a virtual device only through it, and `pactl` needs the property list quoted.
- **The ASR does poorly on short sentences.** A bare "Stop." delivered nothing and a bare "Send." was joined to the sentence before it; both are full sentences now.
- **WICA drops a trigger that arrives during a reaction**, so every scenario and step waits for the agent to settle first, as a person would.
- **The robot has no echo.** The sim daemon's software canceller (`webrtcdsp`) left Ubuntu 24.04, and the real robot cancels its echo in hardware.

In the model's behaviour (open question 3): Haiku plays `say` and an emotion at once (recorded, not failed); meets a dictated note with `noop` (allowed in that step); once called `cancel_command` with the call id stripped of its `toolu_` prefix (recorded, for WICA).

## The computer's echo on Linux

With the room's echo on, the app's own sentences came back as speech inputs. Recorded in CI (`audio.npz`, `EchoRecorder`): the mic hears the echo **1.1 s before** the canceller receives the matching reference, constantly, so it removes nothing (median 0.8 dB). The mic's path (the ALSA plugin over PulseAudio, then the remapped source) delivers about 1.1 s later than the speaker's plays, while the app pairs each mic frame with the newest reference. First fix tried: `PULSE_LATENCY_MSEC=30` in the job. If it holds, the lasting fix in the app — aligning the reference by the devices' timestamps — goes to the audio-processing spec as a later improvement.
